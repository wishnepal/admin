Photorealistic, high detail, appetizing, no plastic AI look.

PROMPT 2

Create a dynamic action shot of the product shown in the reference photo, with its key ingredients and particles bursting and floating around it, frozen mid-air. Dramatic studio lighting, rich texture, true-to-life colors, photorealistic, no over-smoothing.

PROMPT 3

Create a lifestyle shot of a real, natural-looking person enjoying the product shown in the reference photo. Candid mid-action pose, genuine expression, realistic un-retouched skin with visible pores, cinematic lighting, premium advertising look. Keep the packaging accurate to the reference.

PROMPT 4

Create an extreme macro close-up of the product shown in the reference photo, emphasizing its texture and detail, with subtle particles or droplets around it. Shallow depth of field, hyper-real detail, mouth-watering, photorealistic, no AI plastic look.

STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW · EDIT VIDEOS WITH GOOGLE OMNI

Video Clone Prompt

Use Claude + Gemini Omni to recreate any video style with your own script. No editing skills needed.

01
THE WORKFLOW · STEP 1

Find your reference video

Instagram, Pinterest, or YouTube

Find 1-2 motion design videos whose style you love. Screen-record or save them. Also have your script ready — the exact words you want spoken in your video.
WHERE TO LOOK
"kinetic typography reel" "motion design short" "AI video edit style"
Search these on Instagram Reels or YouTube. Save anything that has the energy you want.
02
THE WORKFLOW · STEP 2

Open Claude and paste this prompt

Upload reference + your script

Upload your reference video (or screenshots from it) and paste your script. Then paste the prompt below. Claude will analyze the style and write ready-made scene prompts for you.
Paste this entire prompt into Claude 
I'm attaching a reference video and my script below.
I want to recreate this video's STYLE for my own
script using an AI video generator (Gemini Omni / Veo).
Step 1: Analyze the reference video deeply. Describe:
  - Animation style (2D, 3D, kinetic typography, flat design)
  - How things move (speed, easing, bounce, smooth or snappy)
  - Transitions (cuts, wipes, morphs, zooms)
  - Color palette (exact colors and background)
  - Typography style (bold, thin, animated, where text appears)
  - Camera behavior (static, zoom, pan, shake)
  - Overall mood and pacing (fast, calm, dramatic)
  - Sound feel (whooshes, clicks, bass hits, music style)
Step 2: Break my script into scenes of 8-10 seconds
each (because the AI generates max 10 seconds per clip).
Step 3: For EACH scene, write one detailed prompt that includes:
  - What appears on screen and how it animates
  - The exact text shown on screen (max 5-6 words)
  - Colors, background, and typography matching the reference
  - Camera movement
  - Transition into the next scene
  - Sound effects and music mood
Here is my script:
  [PASTE YOUR SCRIPT HERE]

PRO-LEVEL PROMPT · SCENE-BY-SCENE ANALYZER

Deep-dive prompt: recreate any video shot-for-shot

Paste into Claude or ChatGPT with your reference video

This is the advanced, polished version of your scene-by-scene prompt. It forces the AI to reverse-engineer the video with cinematic precision — timecodes, shot sizes, lens character, light direction, emotion, and ready-to-paste AI video prompts per shot. Use this when you need maximum fidelity.
⚡ Polished Master Prompt — Scene-by-Scene Video Analyzer 
ROLE:
You are a senior film director, cinematographer, and AI video prompt engineer with deep experience in narrative cinema, commercial advertising, and short-form social video. Your job is to reverse-engineer any reference video into production-ready, shot-by-shot AI video prompts that reproduce its look, feel, and rhythm with maximum fidelity.

INPUT:
- Attached: [reference video file OR screenshots of every scene]
- My script / voiceover (optional): [paste below, or write 'no script — match the reference only']
- Target AI video generator: [Sora / Veo 3 / Gemini Omni / Runway Gen-3 / Kling / Pika — pick one, or I'll keep it generator-agnostic]

TASK:
Analyze the reference video SHOT BY SHOT and produce a complete prompt sheet that could recreate it almost identically.

────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 1 — GLOBAL STYLE ANALYSIS
────────────────────────────────────────
Give me a short report covering:
- Genre & mood (e.g., gritty documentary, luxury commercial, cyberpunk short)
- Overall pacing & rhythm (fast cuts, long takes, jump cuts)
- Aspect ratio & frame-rate feel (24fps cinematic vs 60fps crisp)
- Color grade (LUT name if possible — e.g., teal-orange, bleach bypass, warm vintage)
- Camera language (handheld, gimbal, dolly, drone, static tripod)
- Lens character (wide 24mm, normal 50mm, telephoto 85mm, anamorphic flares)
- Lighting philosophy (natural, high-key, low-key, practical-driven, neon)
- Sound design & music feel (if audio is present)
- Reference directors / films / ads this resembles

────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 2 — SHOT-BY-SHOT BREAKDOWN
────────────────────────────────────────
Break the video into individual shots. For EACH shot, output a numbered entry with a timecode and the following 16 fields:

SHOT [##] — [Timecode, e.g., 00:00–00:03]

1. Duration: X.X seconds
2. Shot size: EWS / WS / MS / MCU / CU / ECU / insert
3. Camera angle: eye-level, low, high, dutch, bird's-eye, POV
4. Camera movement: static, pan, tilt, dolly in/out, truck, crane, handheld shake, orbit, zoom
5. Lens & DOF: focal-length guess, shallow/deep focus, bokeh quality
6. Subject: who/what is on screen, wardrobe, props
7. Subject action: exact physical action, pace, gesture
8. Facial expression & emotion: micro-expressions, eye direction, mood
9. Environment & set: location, time of day, weather, background activity
10. Lighting: key direction, fill, rim, practicals, color temperature, shadows, contrast
11. Composition: rule of thirds, symmetry, leading lines, negative space, framing choices
12. Color & palette: dominant hues, accents, skin tones
13. Motion & pace: how the shot breathes, when it cuts in/out
14. Transition out: cut / dissolve / whip-pan / match cut / speed ramp / glitch
15. Audio: SFX, dialogue, music cue, ambience
16. AI video prompt (ready to paste): ONE rich paragraph optimized for an AI video generator, describing everything above in cinematic language (subject, action, camera, lens, lighting, mood, color, motion, style). Write it as a single continuous prompt of 60–120 words. Then, on a new line, add: NEGATIVE PROMPT: [list of things to avoid].

────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 3 — CONTINUITY NOTES
────────────────────────────────────────
- Recurring characters, wardrobe, props, locations
- Consistent color / lighting rules across shots
- Recurring motifs (symbols, camera moves, transitions)

────────────────────────────────────────
STEP 4 — RECREATION PACKAGE
────────────────────────────────────────
- A 'Master Style Prompt' I can prepend to every shot for consistency
- A 'Negative Prompt' list (things to avoid)
- Suggested generation order (which shots to make first for best continuity)

────────────────────────────────────────
OUTPUT FORMAT
────────────────────────────────────────
Use clean markdown. Number every shot. Keep language cinematic and specific — no vague words like 'beautiful' or 'nice'. Always name the camera move, the light direction, and the emotion. If a shot is ambiguous, state your best guess and mark it [guess].

Analyze the video now.

THE WORKFLOW · STEP 4

Generate in Gemini Omni

One scene at a time

Open the Gemini app. Select the video model — Gemini Omni Flash is the newest. Paste one scene prompt, attach your reference video, and hit generate. Repeat for each scene.
PRO TIP
Always attach your reference video or raw footage clip to each generation. Without it, Omni invents the style from scratch instead of matching yours.
05
THE WORKFLOW · STEP 5

Iterate with chat commands

Fix anything in plain language

If a scene looks wrong, just tell Gemini in the chat. It will redo that scene. Keep the best version of each clip.
EXAMPLE FIX COMMANDS
"Make the text bigger" "Slow down the animation" "Change the background color to red" "Add more glitch effect on the transition" "Make the text slam in harder"
06
THE WORKFLOW · STEP 6

Assemble in CapCut

Add music, voiceover, and export

Drop all your scenes into CapCut (or any editor), arrange them in order, add music or voiceover, and export. Done.
Important: If any on-screen text looks messy or wrong, redo that text directly in CapCut or Figma. AI still struggles with perfectly clean text — always double-check before posting.
